Do you know the NAP 15 rules? Do you know who the active voting members are? Do you know the cases they've seen and what they've decided on? Does the NAP 15 have any presence to you except for extremely fundamental rules that appear to be selectively enforced based almost entirely on vibes?

People want:

- ✅ protection  
- ✅ clear expectations  
- ✅ predictable outcomes  
- ✅ a sustainable social environment
- ✅ a server that wins

People *don’t* want:

- ❌ walls of text  
- ❌ unclear rules  
- ❌ asymmetry  
- ❌ loopholes  
- ❌ verbose, boring meetings

- minimizes ambiguity

# 🟥 **1. The Fundamental Problem With NAP 15**

A leading reason that our NAP failed was because it assumes ideal behavior:

- **All R5s are equally active.** They aren’t.  
- **All alliances want to govern.** They don’t.  
- **Systems enforce themselves.** They never do.  
- **Players agree on intent.** They never will.  
- **Everyone cares equally about diplomacy.** They don’t.  

📉 The previous NAP tried to get 15+ alliances to cooperate, vote, deliberate, enforce, and be available around an incredible loose and imprecise unstructured document.

📈 NAP 9 fixes the behavioral issue by shrinking governance to the smallest stable group possible, adding quorum, and introducing representation flexibility.

---

# 🟩 **2. NAP 9’s Core Design Principle**

## ✨ **“As few people as possible, with as much clarity as possible.”**

The goal is **not** maximal inclusion.  
The goal is **maximal functionality**.

A governance system should be:

- 🪶 **small enough** to act quickly  
- 👁️ **clear enough** for half-asleep R5s to understand  
- 🧱 **structured enough** that personal drama cannot break it  
- ⚙️ **simple enough** that enforcement is predictable  

9 alliances → 9 reps → 9 votes → 1 system.

---

# 🟨 **3. Why 9? (The Governance Sweet Spot)**

NAP 9 is built around a well-studied governance principle:

> 📘 **“Decision-making groups degrade after ~9–11 members.”**  
	*(Organizational design, military doctrine, cognitive load research.)*

Why 9 works:

- small enough to coordinate  
- large enough to distribute power   
- resilient during absences  
- stable in disputes  

NAP 15 was too large.  
NAP 5 would be too consolidated.  
**NAP 9 is the Goldilocks zone.**

# 🟦 **4. The Real Goals of a NAP (Most Forget This)**

A NAP is **not**:

- a friendship pact  
- a moral code  
- a punishment ledger  
- a vibes-based honor system  

A NAP **is**:

- ⚖️ a stability engine  
- 🕊️ a conflict minimizer  
- 🗺️ a land-management protocol  
- 🧾 a judicial framework  
- 🔄 a predictable system that outlives its creators  

A good NAP is less about peace and more about **consistent system behavior** even when:

- people vanish  
- diplomacy collapses  
- emotions spike  
- alliances implode
- details are sketchy

# 🟫 **5. Why the System Uses Clerks**

Another major reason NAP 15 failed was because it expect **R5s** to run governance.  

But R5s tend to:

- sleep  
- work  
- disappear  
- get emotional  
- have biases  
- hate paperwork  
- don’t track evidence
- be extremely busy with organizing within their own alliance

So NAP 9 uses:

🗂️ **Clerks — neutral procedural operators**

They:

- handle evidence  
- validate submissions  
- assign case IDs  
- open vote windows  
- track votes  
- archive outcomes
- love praise

### 👩‍⚖️ Council = Judges  
### 👨‍💼 Clerks = Court Staff

This separation:

- prevents emotional decisions  
- prevents evidence tampering  
- keeps procedures consistent  
- makes governance *institutional*, not *personal*

# 🟪 **6. Offenses Must Be Categorized, Not Debated**

Players will argue forever about:

- intent  
- tone  
- emotion  
- “malice”  
- “accidents”  

Humans are terrible at judging intent.  
So NAP 9 uses **behavior categories**:

### Minor → Moderate → Severe

### The rule:

## 🔍 **If behavior fits a category → the penalty applies.**

No psychoanalysis.  
No drama.  
No endless debate threads.
Last effort discretionary powers for extreme cases.

---

# 🟥 **7. Why Tag-Dropping Is the Only Automatic Blacklist Rule**

Tag-dropping:

- evades accountability  
- hides identity  
- breaks diplomacy pathways  
- is almost always malicious  
- can be super destabilizing

Thus:

- **Instance 1 → 48-hour ban**  
- **Instance 2 → permanent blacklist**  

No vote.  
No debate.  
No ambiguity.

People who do this tend to thrive off of the attention and reaction afford to them for their actions. The cruelty and outrage are the point. An immediate and automated system avoids prolonging the attention and minimizes the amount of people who have to talk or think about it.

This mechanism protects the entire server ecosystem without a human element involved in administering highly punishing disciplinary action.

---

# 🟧 **8. Why Land Rules Must Be Strict**

Land is:

- scarce  
- political  
- impactful towards alliance growth
- tied to alliance identity  

NAP 9 treats land like the valuable resource that it is:

- L6 → Top 3 only
- Non-Members lose all land protection  
- Diplomacy is mandatory  
- Surprise takeovers are forbidden  

Super easy emphasis as this eliminates a LOT of potential server drama before it even starts.

---

# 🟥 **9. Why Non-Members Lose Land Protection**

Because halfway-protected alliances:

- abuse diplomacy  
- seed conflict
- frustrate governance
- leech benefits without giving back to the server

### NAP 9 establishes a simple truth:

## 🛑 **You can be protected OR independent — not both.**

If you opt out:

- you lose NAP protection  
- you must drop L6 immediately  
- L1–L5 become seize-at-will  
- you cannot file cases  
- you cannot claim restitution  

This keeps the system clean.

---

# 🟦 **10. Why Voting Is Asynchronous**

NAP 9 respects reality:

- people have jobs  
- people sleep  
- global time zones exist  
- emergencies happen  

So vote windows are:

- ⏳ **24h** → high-impact votes  
- 🕒 **16h** → standard votes  
- ⏱️ **8h** → emergencies  

Quorum = **5/9**.  

Participation is mechanically enforced, but the system keeps ticking at the appropriate rate no matter what happens. 

Stalled votes are extremely rare under this system, which effectively insists on reliability. The easy and regularity of voting means it actually happens, with no uncertainty about if enough Council members are even active enough for a vote to take place.

---

# 🟩 **11. Why ABSTAIN Exists**

ABSTAIN allows:

- conflict-of-interest recusal  
- neutrality  
- uncertainty  
- diplomacy preservation  

Forcing YES/NO creates:

- tension
- complications around result integrity
- pressure to decide on cases your delegates have no consensus on
- strong outputs based on dispassionate inputs

ABSTAIN lessens the headache around many voting cases for Council members and does a lot to stabilize the vote ecosystem with a very small feature.

---

# 🟦 **12. Why Evidence Rules Are Harsh**

Because:
  
- DMs lie  
- timestamps get cropped  
- context disappears  
- narratives get rewritten  

So evidence MUST be:

- unedited  
- timestamped  
- clear  
- complete  
- submitted in `#evidence`  

Invalid evidence → invalid case. Evidence sources which are cross-confirmational are very credible. Evidence sources which are only single-party-view cannot be trusted as authoritative. Cases based on shaky foundations decrease trust and integrity in the system. Without the trust of the NAPs literally hundreds of beneficiaries, the system falls into disuse.

---

# 🟧 **13. Why Escalation Logic Is Strict**

To stop:

- chronic offenders  
- loophole abuse  
- “but last time…” arguments  

Escalation is automatic:

- 2 Minors → Moderate  
- 2 Moderates → Severe  
- Evasion → Severe  
- Probation violation → automatic escalation  

I'm going to be honest with you. Yes, accidents happen. But it actually is not that hard to not fuck up if you pay any attention to actions that have potential consequences. And if you need to touch the hot iron for your brain to truly internalize that you must be careful when handling the hot iron, the NAP ensures that the temperature is exactly hot enough to leave such an impression.

---

# 🟪 **14. Why the NAP Needs Amendments**

Servers change.  
Alliances rise and fall.

A static NAP becomes ineffectual. An ineffectual NAP becomes irrelevant. An irrelevant NAP decreases the overall sense of server cohesion. A slow death, but a certain one.

NAP 9 uses:

- supermajority  
- notice period  
- clear structure  
- archives  
- emergency amendments  

Adaptive governance IS stable governance.

---

# 🟩 **15. Why the System Works**

NAP 9 is grounded in real behavioral truths:

1. 💤 People default to laziness → make it simple  
2. 🧍 People default to self-interest → make it symmetric  
3. 🎭 Leaders default to politics → make it procedural  
4. 🎨 Evidence defaults to manipulation → make it strict  
5. 🚨 Emergencies default to chaos → formalize responses  
6. 🔥 Drama defaults to escalation → structure the pathway  
7. 👥 Stability defaults to small groups → keep Council small  

NAP 9 is engineered around **how people actually behave**, not how we wish they behaved.
